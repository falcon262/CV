## Context and the problem

IFRS 9 and IFRS 16 are accounting standards with regulatory weight. IFRS 9 asks banks to account for expected credit loss on their lending, and IFRS 16 changes how leases are accounted for, down to the amortisation schedule for each lease.

At Consolidated Bank Ghana and Fidelity Bank Ghana these regulatory calculations were done manually. At PwC Ghana I worked as an engineer on the automation that replaced them.

## What I did

The work covers two engines:

- an IFRS 9 impairment engine computing expected credit loss, supported by probability-of-default calibration validation reporting
- an IFRS 16 lease accounting engine producing lease amortisation schedules

Probability-of-default estimates sit underneath expected credit loss, so the IFRS 9 engine comes with reporting that validates how well those estimates are calibrated. On the IFRS 16 side, every lease needs its own amortisation schedule, and the engine produces them.

Both are built on .NET with ABP Framework, with an Angular front end and SQL Server, as regulatory compliance automation for the two banks.

## Outcome

Manual regulatory calculations were replaced with automated, repeatable engines: expected credit loss impairment under IFRS 9, and lease accounting under IFRS 16. The same inputs produce the same results every time, and the probability-of-default calibration can be checked through its own validation reporting.
